EmpiricalBench judge audit

How this audit works

Each item is one Pareto frontier from one search of on one problem, at one of two checkpoints: into the search, or the end of the one-hour search. The LLM judge () decided whether the frontier contains an exact recovery of the target relationship. You decide the same thing, without seeing its answer first.

  • Recovered: some equation has exactly the accepted variable dependence (fitted constants are free only where the target has a free constant). Near: the essential structure with a genuine extra/missing term, or an approximation. Miss: neither. Only Recovered counts in the paper's metric, so agreement is scored on Recovered vs. not.
  • Click a frontier row to mark the equation your decision rests on (optional).
  • The judge's verdict appears once you label. Your first label is kept as the blind label even if you change your mind afterwards; on a disagreement you can record who was right.
  • The queue order is a fixed stratified shuffle: every 18 consecutive items cover each problem at each checkpoint once, so any prefix you finish is a balanced random sample.
  • Numbers are rounded for display. An exact value prints short (2, 1.5); a rounded one keeps all its digits (1.000, 1.500) and is tinted. Toggle Raw for the saved strings, which are authoritative.
  • The simplified line is a sympy rewrite (simplify + expanded logs) meant only as a reading aid. Except for Bode it assumes positive inputs, which holds on these datasets.
  • Early-checkpoint verdicts are what the solve-over-time figure plots: recovered iff the binary-searched first recovery is at or before the checkpoint. Many of those frontiers were never shown to the judge; the verdict panel says whether it is direct or implied.
  • Decisions autosave in this browser (localStorage). Use Export to get a JSON file to send back; Import restores it.
