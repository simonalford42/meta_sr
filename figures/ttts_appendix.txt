\subsection{Allocating reevaluations with top-two Thompson sampling}
\label{app:ttts}

Choosing which programs to reevaluate is a \emph{best-arm identification} problem. Each program is an arm, and each extra evaluation seed is a noisy measurement of that arm's mean fitness. The goal is to identify the best arm, not to maximize the fitness observed along the way. We use top-two Thompson sampling (TTTS) \citep{russo2020simple}, which is proven asymptotically optimal for this problem: among allocation rules that give a fraction $\beta$ of measurements to the best arm, it identifies the best arm at the fastest possible rate, and with $\beta = 1/2$ its rate is within a factor of two of the optimum over all rules. TTTS refines Thompson sampling (TS), which measures each arm with the posterior probability that it is the best. TS is designed to maximize cumulative reward. As a result, once the posterior favors one arm, TS measures that arm almost exclusively, and competing arms get too few measurements to be ruled out. TTTS fixes this by giving part of the budget to the strongest challenger. At each step, TTTS:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Samples $\tilde\theta$ from the posterior and lets $I = \arg\max_i \tilde\theta_i$.
    \item With probability $\beta$, measures arm $I$.
    \item Otherwise, resamples until the argmax is some $J \neq I$, and measures arm $J$.
\end{enumerate}

We model the fitness from each seed as Gaussian with a known noise level $\sigma$ shared by all programs. Under a flat prior, the posterior for program $i$ is $\mathcal{N}(\mu_i, \sigma^2 / N_i)$, where $\mu_i$ is its mean fitness over $N_i$ seeds. Instead of simulating the steps above, we compute the TTTS measurement probabilities exactly. The probability that TS picks arm $i$ is
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_i = \int \phi_i(x) \prod_{j \neq i} \Phi_j(x) \, dx,
\end{equation}
where $\phi_i$ is the posterior density of arm $i$ and $\Phi_j$ is the posterior CDF of arm $j$. We evaluate this integral with Gauss--Hermite quadrature. The TTTS probabilities then follow in closed form \citep{russo2020simple}:
\begin{equation}
    \psi_i = \alpha_i \Big( \beta + (1 - \beta) \sum_{j \neq i} \frac{\alpha_j}{1 - \alpha_j} \Big).
\end{equation}

TTTS is sequential: it updates the posterior after every measurement. Our evaluations are expensive and run in parallel, so we use a batched version with a fixed budget of $B$ reevaluation seeds per generation. Each generation, we compute $\psi$ over the whole archive, draw $B$ programs from $\psi$ with replacement, and run the resulting seeds in parallel with the evaluation of new offspring. When they finish, we update $\mu_i$ and $N_i$ for each reevaluated program. We use $\beta = 1/2$ and a fixed $\sigma$ estimated offline from an earlier run.
