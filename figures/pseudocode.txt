	Add a random constant to a randomly chosen subtree.
Survival
	Replace the oldest population member.
Selection
	Tournament selection with adaptive parsimony that encourages equally frequent selection across complexity levels, with running search statistics to track the frequency of selection at different complexity levels.
Loss

Evolved:

Mutation

	With probability 0.5, cyclically shift its variable indices by a random offset.
	Choose a random target subtree t and replace it with t + m, t * m, or t / (1 - m), chosen randomly.

Survival
	Replace the population member with the highest weighted sum of normalized age (0.75 weight) and cost (0.25 weight), where cost is normalized loss plus a complexity penalty.

Selection
	Use a tournament candidate pool 50% larger than the default.
	With 10% probability, select the member from the pool with the rarest complexity.
	Otherwise, conduct tournament selection with adaptive parsimony, with an extra penalty to candidates with the same complexity and nearly the same loss as a better-ranked candidate.

Loss
	Fit a scale and offset to the expression's predictions, then measure NRMSE of the fitted predictions.
	Add a small term (weight 1/256) with the bounded NRMSE of the original predictions, to favor expressions that already have the right scale and offset.

Evolved (runs/150815-simplify-30-best2/best_bundles/best_final.jl):

Loss/cost:
	Loss is mean squared error.
	Cost is one minus squared correlation between predictions and targets, plus a bounded robust error term (weight 0.5) and a small complexity penalty (weight 1e-6).
	The robust term is one minus exp(-mean(log(1 + squared error / target variance))).

Survival:
	From the merged set of population and children, keep the best (lowest cost) expression at each complexity on the cost-complexity Pareto frontier first.
	Fill remaining slots with the best expression at each complexity level not on the frontier, visiting simpler levels first.
	Repeat with the second-best expression at each complexity, then the third-best, and so on until all slots are filled.

Selection:
	With 50% probability, choose a complexity level with probability inversely proportional to its rank from simplest to most complex. Sample up to seven members at that level and select the youngest whose cost is close to the sample's best.
	Tighten this cost tolerance as the search progresses, from 0.021 to 0.001 times the larger of one and the absolute best sampled cost.
	Otherwise, sample up to eight members and select the one with the lowest cost plus an age penalty of up to 0.10 times the larger of one and its absolute cost.
	Sample with replacement in both modes.

Mutation:
	With 35% probability, perturb a random constant with multiplicative and additive Gaussian noise, choosing a coarse or ten-times-finer step with equal probability. If there are no constants, replace a random leaf with a random variable or constant.
	With 45% probability, replace a random subtree with either a random variable or constant, or a random unary or binary operator with terminal inputs, with equal probability.
	Otherwise, wrap a random subtree in a random unary or binary operator, using a random variable or constant as the other input for binary operators.

Acceptance:
	Accept a child if it improves cost plus a complexity penalty (0.02 * complexity / maximum size).
	Also accept if if no current population member has both equal or lower cost and equal or lower complexity.
	Otherwise, accept probabilistically, penalizing increased cost and complexity relative to the parent and reducing exploration as the search progresses.

Crossover:
	Find subtrees in one parent whose predictions correlate with the other parent's residual errors.
	Match promising donor subtrees to structurally compatible sites in the recipient parent.
	Try subtree replacement, left or right child import, operator replacement, and arithmetic combinations where available.
	Keep the valid child with the best sampled correlation-and-error score plus a complexity penalty, provided it is not too much worse than the recipient parent.
	Repeat in the opposite direction for a possible second child.

Update population:
	Every two or three completed population sweeps, send each population's best (lowest cost) member to the next population in a ring to replace the destination's worst member.
	Every second migration period, also send an expression from the archive to the population whose best member is worst.

Update state:
	Store in the archive a Pareto frontier over expression loss and complexity.


Evolved (runs/229869/best_bundles/best_final.jl; 150815 simplify more, through generation 90):

Loss/cost:
	Loss is mean squared error.
	Cost is one minus squared correlation between predictions and targets, plus a bounded robust error term (weight 0.5) and a small complexity penalty (weight 1e-6).
	The robust term is one minus exp(-mean(log(1 + squared error / target variance))), with target variance floored at 1e-12.

Survival:
	Merge the population and children, discard members with non-finite loss or cost, and deduplicate exact expressions, keeping the lowest-cost copy (breaking ties by lower loss, then younger age).
	Keep the cost-complexity Pareto frontier first, visiting simpler expressions first.
	Fill remaining slots with the best expression at each remaining complexity level, visiting simpler levels first, then with the remaining expressions in order of increasing cost.
	If there are too few unique expressions, pad with copies of the first survivor.

Selection:
	Sample up to eight members with finite loss and cost, with replacement.
	With 55% probability, choose from the sample's cost-complexity Pareto frontier, with probability inversely proportional to rank from simplest to most complex.
	Otherwise, select the youngest of the three lowest-cost sampled members (or all sampled members if fewer than three).

Mutation:
	With 35% probability, choose a random leaf. Perturb a constant c to c * (1 + 0.5 * Gaussian noise) + 0.1 * independent Gaussian noise, or replace a variable with a random variable or constant.
	With 45% probability, replace a random subtree with either a random variable or constant, or a random unary or binary operator with terminal inputs, with equal probability.
	Otherwise, wrap a random subtree in a random unary or binary operator, using a random variable or constant as the other input for binary operators.
	Retry invalid proposals up to eight attempts, choosing the mutation type again on each attempt.

Acceptance:
	Reject children with invalid trees or non-finite loss or cost; accept valid children when the parent's loss or cost is non-finite.
	Accept a child if it improves cost plus a complexity penalty (0.02 * complexity / maximum size).
	Also accept if no current population member has both equal or lower cost and equal or lower complexity, with at least one strict improvement.
	Otherwise, accept probabilistically, penalizing increased cost and complexity relative to the parent and reducing exploration as the search progresses.

Crossover:
	On up to 64 randomly sampled training rows, rank donor subtrees by correlation with the recipient parent's residual errors and targets, with a preference for smaller subtrees.
	Keep the five best donors and the twelve best donor-site pairs, favoring similar subtree sizes.
	Try subtree replacement, addition or subtraction according to residual correlation, multiplication, and division where available; reject invalid children and exact copies of either parent.
	Keep the child with the best sampled correlation-and-robust-error score plus a complexity penalty, if it is smaller than the recipient parent or its score is at most 0.12 worse.
	If this succeeds, repeat in the opposite direction for a possible second child. Otherwise, try up to 24 subtree swaps ranked by size and arity compatibility.

Update population:
	At the end of each pass over all populations, send each population's best member to the next population in a ring.
	Replace the destination's worst member only if the migrant is a valid, new expression and has lower cost (breaking ties by lower complexity).

Update state:
	Merge the archive with all population members having finite loss and cost, and rebuild the loss-complexity Pareto frontier.
	Keep expressions in increasing complexity only when loss strictly improves, preferring older expressions on exact complexity-and-loss ties.
