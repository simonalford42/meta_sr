Baseline:

Mutation
	wrap a random subtree with addition of a random constant
Survival
	replace the oldest population member
Selection
	tournament selection with adaptive parsimony — tournament selection such that we select equally often among all complexity levels
Loss
	mean squared error

Evolved:

Mutation
	Take a random subtree from the expression and shuffle its variables with probability 0.5, and return uniform over either expr + subtree, expr * subtree, or expr / (1 - subtree)

Survival
	Replace the population member with the highest combination of age (0.75 weight) and cost (0.25 weight), where cost is the expression's normalized loss plus a complexity penalty.

Selection
	Tournament selection with 50% larger candidate pool than default
	10% chance of selecting member of rarest complexity in the candidate pool
	Otherwise, conduct tournament selection with adaptive parsimony, with a small added penalty to candidates with near-identical complexity and loss to other candidates

Loss
	Fit a scale and offset to the expression's predictions, then measure NRMSE of the fitted predictions
	Add a small term (weight 1/256) with the bounded NRMSE of the original predictions, to favor expressions that already have the right scale and offset.


Proposed paper version:

Baseline:

Mutation
	Add a random constant to a randomly chosen subtree.
Survival
	Replace the oldest population member.
Selection
	Tournament selection with adaptive parsimony: penalize common complexity levels to encourage a wider range of expression sizes.
Loss
	Mean squared error.

Evolved:

Mutation
	Copy a random variable-containing subtree m, preferring compound subtrees that fit the size limit. With probability 0.5, shift its variable indices cyclically.
	Choose a random target subtree t and replace it with t + m, t * m, or t / (1 - m), with probabilities 32.5%, 32.5%, and 35% when all three forms are available and fit the size limit.

Survival
	Scale age and cost to [0, 1] within the eligible population, then replace the member with the highest weighted sum: 0.75 * age + 0.25 * cost.
	Higher age means older; higher cost means worse normalized loss plus a complexity penalty.

Selection
	Use a tournament candidate pool about 50% larger than the baseline.
	With 10% probability, select the candidate with the rarest complexity level in the search, breaking ties by lower selection score.
	Otherwise, use tournament selection with adaptive parsimony, with an extra penalty for candidates that have the same complexity and nearly the same loss as a better-ranked candidate.

Loss
	Fit a scale and offset to the expression's predictions, then measure their normalized root mean squared error (NRMSE).
	Add a small bounded NRMSE term for the original predictions (weight 1/256), favoring expressions that already have the right scale and offset.
	Normalize errors by the target standard deviation. This mainly rewards the right functional shape, while still encouraging accurate original predictions.
