\subsection{MIPS}
Mechanistic-Interpretability-based Program Synthesis (MIPS) \citep{mips} extracts executable programs from recurrent neural networks trained on algorithmic tasks. The original pipeline searches for small, accurate networks, simplifies their representations, and uses Boolean or integer autoencoders to identify discrete hidden states. Regression then recovers symbolic expressions for the state-transition and output functions, which are assembled into Python programs. The authors report successful synthesis on 32 of 62 algorithmic tasks. We continue from the released pretrained networks and integer-state extraction pipeline, focusing on improving the symbolic-regression stage. Each network yields one scalar regression problem per hidden-state coordinate and output coordinate: predicting the next-state coordinate from the previous state and current input, or predicting the output coordinate from the current state. Thus, recovering an algorithmic task requires solving all of its component symbolic regression problems.

\paragraph{Refining the state discretization.}
Of the 30 tasks reported as unsolved by MIPS, eight have deterministic state-transition and output relations under our reproduction of the original encoding, making them candidates for recovery through improved symbolic regression. These tasks contribute 22 scalar regression problems. The remaining 22 tasks are initially excluded: 17 have conflicting targets under the original state encoding, and five exceed the integer encoder implementation's supported hidden-state dimensionality. To expand the candidate set, we refine the state discretization to resolve encoding conflicts.

The original integer encoding rounds continuous lattice coordinates $Z$ to $\mathrm{round}(Z)$. This can merge distinct states whose transitions or outputs differ, producing contradictory regression targets that no deterministic expression can recover exactly. We retain the learned lattice basis but refine its resolution using $\mathrm{round}(kZ)$, testing powers of four from $k=1$ through $k=2^{24}$. We also test an encoding that concatenates the coarse state with a quantized within-cell residual. Among the 17 tasks with conflicting original encodings, the sweep finds conflict-free representations for ten, of which six retain at most 1,024 observed states. Subsequent artifact construction and validation leave four additional tasks requiring symbolic regression: Parity-Last-4 ($k=4$), Div-7 ($k=16$), Div-5 ($k=256$), and Alternating-Last-3 ($k=2^{22}$). Of the other two compact candidates, Parity-of-Index is solved by rounded linear regression, while Balanced-Parenthesis remains conflicted in the rebuilt artifacts and is excluded. The four retained tasks have deterministic relations across the generated training and held-out data and contribute 17 additional scalar regression problems. This expands the candidate set from eight to twelve previously unsolved algorithmic tasks, comprising 39 scalar regression problems. Determinism makes exact relation recovery possible on the observed data but does not guarantee that symbolic regression will find compact expressions or that the resulting programs will generalize to unseen sequences.

\paragraph{Meta-training tasks and objective.}
We evolve PySR on 51 scalar regression problems from 17 algorithmic tasks. These comprise the 39 problems from the twelve previously unsolved tasks described above and 12 problems from five tasks reported as solved by MIPS. Of these five, absolute value, absolute difference, and addition modulo three contribute seven scalar problems and are recovered using symbolic regression in our reproduction. The other two, Parity-Last-2 and Previous-Equals-Current (called Unique2 in the released code), contribute five scalar problems; although our reproduction does not recover them, we retain their paper-reported solved status. All 51 problems are used for meta-training, while additional-recovery counts include only the twelve previously unsolved tasks. Fitness is the exact-recovery rate averaged uniformly over the 51 scalar problems and three search seeds. A scalar problem is recovered when at least one expression in the discovered frontier matches every entry of its complete relation within absolute tolerance $10^{-9}$ and zero relative tolerance. We evolve for 20 generations with ten offspring per generation.
