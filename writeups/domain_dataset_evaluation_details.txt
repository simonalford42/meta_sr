\paragraph{SRBench.}
We evaluate symbolic regression on the 133 ground-truth problems in SRBench, comprising 119 Feynman problems and 14 Strogatz problems. For each trial, we sample at most 1,000 observations and use a seeded $80/20$ training--validation split. Noise experiments add Gaussian noise to the training targets with standard deviation equal to a specified fraction of their root-mean-square magnitude, using noise levels $0$, $0.001$, $0.01$, and $0.1$; validation targets remain unperturbed. We assess equation recovery across the complete discovered Pareto frontier. A trial is successful if at least one expression passes the validation-$R^2$ threshold of $0.5$ and matches the reference equation under the symbolic checker's allowance for an additive or multiplicative constant. Recovery rates are averaged over problems and stochastic trials. We retain all 133 problems in the evaluation denominator, including three inverse-trigonometric targets that are outside the configured search vocabulary.

\paragraph{EmpiricalBench.}
We evaluate on nine historical empirical relationships: Hubble, Kepler, Newton, Bode, Leavitt, Schechter, ideal gas, Planck, and Rydberg. The datasets preserve the benchmark's observation noise and target transformations, including logarithmic targets where specified, while inputs retain their original scales. Each search uses all available observations for fitting and assessment, reflecting the objective of recovering the underlying relationship from a fixed dataset. No additional target noise is introduced. Recovery is assessed across the discovered frontier using dataset-specific reference families: fitted constants are permitted where the reference allows them, while fixed powers and relative coefficients must match. The reviewed portfolio results use LLM-assisted equation assessment with documented manual corrections, and exclude near matches. This structural assessment is particularly important for Planck and Rydberg, for which predictive accuracy alone does not establish recovery. We report recovery frequency across independent trials and, for restart portfolios, cumulative recovery as a function of search time excluding warm-up.

\paragraph{MIPS.}
We formulate program extraction from recurrent neural networks as symbolic regression over the discrete transition relations produced by MIPS. After mapping continuous hidden states to integer lattice coordinates, each network is decomposed into scalar prediction problems: predicting a next-state coordinate from the previous state and current input, or predicting an output coordinate from the current state. Search uses arithmetic and protected discrete operators, including modular arithmetic, floor division, comparisons, and Boolean operations. Because the objective is exact reconstruction of the observed relation, fitting and intermediate scoring use the same selected rows. A candidate is counted as recovered only if it matches every row of the complete, uncapped relation, with absolute tolerance $10^{-9}$ and zero relative tolerance. Encodings that assign conflicting targets to identical inputs cannot receive an exact-recovery score. We additionally measure the fraction of correctly predicted entries to characterize partial solutions. These scores assess scalar relation recovery; they do not by themselves establish correctness on unseen input sequences.

\paragraph{NeuronBench.}
We use a fully observable adaptation of NeuronBench containing six membrane-dynamics worlds: \texttt{z\_rebound}, \texttt{h\_sag}, \texttt{na\_fatigue}, \texttt{ca\_rebound}, \texttt{d\_type}, and \texttt{textbook\_M}. Each task exposes the external current, membrane voltage, and all channel open fractions, and asks the symbolic regressor to recover the membrane vector field $\mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}t$. This converts the benchmark into a noiseless supervised equation-discovery problem without hidden gating-state inference or active experiment selection. We generate independent scrambled-Sobol designs containing 1,024 training states and 16,384 held-out states, spanning external currents in $[-40, 20]$, voltages in $[-95, 60]$, and channel open fractions in $[0, 1]$. Targets are divided by their training-set root-mean-square magnitude for numerical conditioning, and the search vocabulary contains addition, subtraction, and multiplication. Recovery is determined numerically over the entire discovered frontier: a trial succeeds if any expression achieves held-out RMSE divided by the held-out target RMS of at most $10^{-6}$. We also distinguish near-exact solutions at $10^{-3}$ and close solutions at $5 \times 10^{-2}$.
